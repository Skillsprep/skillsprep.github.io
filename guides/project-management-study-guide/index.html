Project Management

Project Management Study Guide: Concepts That Matter

Published Aug 22, 2026 Last reviewed Oct 18, 2026 2 min read By the SkillPrep editorial team

Unofficial study resource. Written independently of any exam provider, employer or software vendor.

Project management interviews and skills tests mix methodology knowledge with scenario judgment. Unlike coding topics, many answers hinge on choosing the best-practice response rather than a strictly right one.

Methodology knowledge

Be crystal clear on the differences:

AspectAgile/ScrumWaterfall
PlanningIncremental, per sprintUpfront, comprehensive
RequirementsExpected to evolveFixed at start
DeliveryWorking increments earlyFinal product at end
Best forUncertain/changing scopeStable, regulated scope

Know Scrum roles: Product Owner (prioritizes backlog), Scrum Master (removes impediments, facilitates), and the Developers (self-managing; older editions of the Scrum Guide called them the Development Team).

Key frameworks to recognize

  • Iron Triangle — Scope, Time, Cost (with Quality in the center). Changing one affects the others.
  • RACI matrix — Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. Exactly one A per task.
  • Critical Path — the longest sequence of dependent tasks; it determines the project's minimum duration.
  • Risk matrix — Probability × Impact. High/high risks get mitigation plans.
  • Triple constraint trade-offs — scenario: deadline moved up → cut scope or add resources.

Scenario question strategy

Many scenario questions describe a project problem and ask what the PM should do first. Best-practice answers usually follow this order:

  1. Analyze/assess the situation (gather facts, check impact)
  2. Communicate with stakeholders
  3. Then act (escalate, change plan, add resources)
Pattern Answers that immediately escalate to management or blame team members are rarely the best first step. Answers starting with analysis or communication are usually the better choice.

Practice questions

Question 1 of 3Frameworks

In a RACI matrix, how many people should be Accountable for a single task?

Why: "Accountable" means owns the outcome. With multiple A's, responsibility diffuses — the core principle of RACI is single accountability per task.
Question 2 of 3Agile

Who is responsible for prioritizing the product backlog in Scrum?

Why: The Product Owner owns the backlog and its ordering by business value. The Scrum Master facilitates the process but doesn't prioritize, and the Developers decide how much work to pull into a sprint.
Question 3 of 3Scheduling

What does the critical path of a project determine?

Why: The critical path is the longest chain of dependent tasks with zero slack. Any delay on it delays the whole project — hence it sets the minimum timeline.

Final tips

  • If you have studied for PMP or CAPM, much of this will feel familiar — it covers similar ground
  • Focus on "what should the PM do FIRST" patterns
  • Brush up on the Scrum events: Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum (the standup), Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective
