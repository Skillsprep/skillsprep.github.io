Digital Marketing Study Guide: SEO, Funnels & Metrics

14 min read 20 Questions Published Aug 8, 2026 Reviewed Oct 18, 2026

Unofficial study resource. Written independently of any exam provider, employer or software vendor.

Digital marketing covers a broad surface: SEO, paid media, email, analytics, and strategy. Interviews and skills tests tend toward definitions applied to realistic campaign scenarios.

SEO essentials

  • On-page SEO — title tags, meta descriptions, headings, internal links, keyword usage
  • Off-page SEO — backlinks and mentions from other sites (a major ranking signal)
  • Technical SEO — site speed, mobile-friendliness, crawlability, sitemaps, structured data
  • CTR = clicks ÷ impressions; Bounce rate = in Google Analytics 4, the percentage of sessions that were not engaged (older GA3 definition was single-page sessions ÷ all sessions).
Common question "What most improves a page's authority?" → quality backlinks. "What helps search engines understand page structure?" → heading tags / semantic HTML / structured data.

Funnel metrics

StageGoalTypical metrics
AwarenessReach new audiencesImpressions, reach, CTR
ConsiderationEngage prospectsTime on site, pages/session, video views
ConversionDrive actionConversion rate, CPA, ROAS
RetentionRepeat businessRetention rate, LTV, churn

Know the formulas: Conversion Rate = Conversions ÷ Visitors, ROAS = Revenue ÷ Ad Spend, CPA = Spend ÷ Conversions.

A/B testing basics

Expect one or two questions on experimentation:

  • Test one variable at a time (subject line, CTA color, headline)
  • Reach statistical significance before deciding — usually 95% confidence
  • Run tests for full business cycles to avoid day-of-week bias
  • Don't stop early when one variant "looks" ahead — that inflates false positives

SEO in four layers

Search engine optimisation is easier to reason about when you split it into four layers. Problems in a lower layer limit what the upper layers can achieve.

LayerQuestion it answersExamples
TechnicalCan search engines reach and understand the pages?Crawlable links, sensible robots rules, working XML sitemap, fast mobile-friendly pages, HTTPS, no duplicate URLs for the same content
On-pageDoes each page clearly say what it is about?Descriptive title, one main heading, meaningful subheadings, descriptive image alt text, internal links with clear anchor text
ContentDoes the page satisfy the searcher's intent?Original, accurate, complete answers matched to what the person wants to do
LinksDo other sites trust and reference this page?Earned mentions from relevant sites, not purchased link schemes

Search intent is a useful idea. Someone searching "how to tie a tie" wants instructions; someone searching "buy silk tie" wants a shop. A product page will struggle to serve the first query however well it is tuned, and a long article will serve the second badly. A good exercise: take one keyword, read what kinds of pages rank, and decide what intent they satisfy before writing anything.

Common mistakes: stuffing a keyword repeatedly, publishing several thin pages for near-identical queries (they compete with each other), accidentally blocking important pages from crawling, and expecting overnight results. SEO outcomes take time and no one can guarantee a position.

Interview reasoning If traffic drops after a site redesign, check the technical layer first: changed URLs without redirects, pages accidentally set to not be indexed, or blocked crawling.

Funnel metrics with worked arithmetic

Take one small paid campaign. Spend: 2,000. Impressions: 50,000. Clicks: 1,000. Purchases: 40. Revenue: 3,000.

MetricFormulaResult
CTRclicks / impressions1,000 / 50,000 = 2%
CPCspend / clicks2,000 / 1,000 = 2.00
Conversion ratepurchases / clicks40 / 1,000 = 4%
CACspend / new customers2,000 / 40 = 50
Average order valuerevenue / purchases3,000 / 40 = 75
ROASrevenue / ad spend3,000 / 2,000 = 1.5

ROAS of 1.5 sounds positive, but it measures revenue, not profit. If the product's gross margin is 40%, the gross profit is 3,000 x 0.40 = 1,200, which is less than the 2,000 spent, so this campaign loses money on the first sale. It might still make sense only if customers buy again, which is why CAC is compared with customer lifetime value.

Watch the denominators. Conversion rate can be measured per click, per session or per visitor, and numbers are not comparable across definitions. Also remember funnel stages multiply: doubling CTR with the same conversion rate doubles purchases, but a click-bait ad that raises CTR while lowering conversion rate may achieve nothing.

Interview reasoning When asked to improve results, locate the weakest stage first. Low CTR points to the ad or targeting; good CTR but low conversion points to the landing page or offer.

A/B testing and attribution

An A/B test shows two versions to comparable random groups at the same time and compares an outcome. Rules that keep it honest:

  • Change one thing. If the headline and the button colour both change, you cannot say which mattered.
  • Decide the metric and the sample size before starting. The required sample depends on your current rate, the smallest improvement worth detecting and the confidence you want; calculators exist for this.
  • Do not peek and stop early. Checking repeatedly and stopping the first time one version looks ahead greatly raises the chance of a false win.
  • Run across whole weeks if behaviour differs by day.

Example: version A converts 40 of 1,000 visitors (4.0%), version B converts 44 of 1,000 (4.4%). That looks like a 10% relative lift, but a difference of four people is well within random noise at this size. Statistical significance indicates how unlikely a difference is to be chance alone; it does not tell you the difference is large or valuable.

Attribution is deciding which touchpoints deserve credit for a conversion. A customer might see a social ad, later search the brand, then click an email. Last-click gives all credit to the email, first-click to the social ad, and linear splits it equally. No model is simply correct; each favours certain channels, so compare more than one view and avoid cutting top-of-funnel activity just because last-click shows it as zero.

Email compliance, practice and glossary

Email rules differ by country and change over time, so check the law that applies to your recipients. The ideas that appear almost everywhere are consistent:

  • Have a lawful basis for contacting people, often clear consent, and keep a record of it.
  • Identify yourself honestly: accurate sender name and non-misleading subject line.
  • Include a clear, working way to unsubscribe and honour requests promptly.
  • Collect only the data you need and protect it.

Buying a list of addresses is risky precisely because those people never agreed to hear from you. It also damages deliverability, since complaints push your messages toward spam folders.

How to practise: pick a fictional shop and invent campaign numbers, then calculate every metric above and say which stage you would test first. Write a one-line test hypothesis such as "Changing the button text to name the benefit will raise the click rate on the landing page." Outline an on-page checklist for one real article.

Glossary: impression is one display of an ad. Landing page is where a click arrives. Bounce means leaving after one page. Retargeting shows ads to people who previously visited. UTM parameters are tags added to a URL to identify the traffic source.

Putting it together: a diagnosis walk-through

Interview questions often hand you a problem and ask what you would do. Use the funnel as a map and let the numbers choose the stage.

Scenario: an online shop spends 3,000 on ads and gets 1,500 clicks and 30 orders. Conversion rate is 30 / 1,500 = 2%. CAC is 3,000 / 30 = 100. Average order value is 60. Even before margin, each customer costs more to acquire than their first order brings in. There are only three levers: pay less per click, convert more of the clicks, or raise the value per customer.

LeverChangeNew CAC
Convert moreConversion rate 2% to 3% (45 orders)3,000 / 45 = 66.67
Cheaper clicksCPC from 2.00 to 1.50 (spend 2,250 for the same 1,500 clicks)2,250 / 30 = 75
Raise order valueBundles lift AOV from 60 to 80CAC unchanged; revenue rises

Notice that a one-point rise in conversion rate improved CAC more than a 25% cut in click cost. Small gains lower in the funnel compound, which is why conversion work is often tested first. Still, you would run it as an A/B test on the landing page, not assume the improvement.

Common mistakes to avoid in answers:

  • Quoting a metric without stating its formula or denominator.
  • Declaring a winner from a tiny sample or after a few days.
  • Treating correlation as proof: sales rose in the month you changed the headline, but there was also a festival or a discount.
  • Optimising one number (clicks) while ignoring the business outcome (profit).

Finish any recommendation with what you would measure next and what result would make you change your mind. That shows structured, evidence-based thinking. For example: "I would test the landing page headline for two full weeks, judge it on orders per visitor, and keep the current page if the difference is within noise."

Practical learning exercises

  • Exercise 1 (SEO Audit): Pick a web page. Write out a new optimized Title tag (under 60 characters) and Meta Description (under 155 characters) targeting a specific long-tail keyword.
  • Exercise 2 (Campaign Math): If you spend $500 on ads, get 10,000 impressions, 200 clicks, and 5 sales, calculate the CTR, CPC, Conversion Rate, and CPA.
  • Exercise 3 (Email Marketing): Draft a promotional email. Ensure it has a compelling subject line, a clear CTA, and complies with CAN-SPAM (e.g., includes an unsubscribe link and physical address).

Practice questions

Question 1 of 20SEO

Which factor most directly increases a website's authority in search?

Why: Backlinks act as "votes of confidence" and remain among the strongest authority signals. Volume of pages or posts without quality doesn't move authority. (Scores such as Moz's Domain Authority are third-party estimates, not Google ranking factors.)
Question 2 of 20Metrics

An ad campaign spent $2,000 and generated $8,000 in attributed revenue. What is the ROAS?

Why: ROAS = Revenue ÷ Ad Spend = 8000 ÷ 2000 = 4. The $6,000 figure is revenue minus spend, not ROAS. Whether 4:1 is "good" depends on your margins — compare ROAS with your break-even ROAS (1 ÷ profit margin).
Question 3 of 20Testing

What is the main reason to avoid stopping an A/B test early?

Why: Early results fluctuate randomly ("peeking problem"). Stopping as soon as one variant leads dramatically increases the chance of declaring a winner that doesn't actually exist.
Question 4 of 20SEO

Which of these is an off-page SEO factor?

Why: Off-page SEO refers to actions taken outside of your own website to impact your rankings, primarily building high-quality backlinks.
Question 5 of 20Metrics

What does Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) measure?

Why: CAC calculates how much a company spends to acquire a single new customer, a vital metric for determining marketing profitability.
Question 6 of 20SEO

Which of these is an 'on-page' SEO factor?

Why: On-page SEO refers to optimizations made directly within the website's HTML and content.
Question 7 of 20Metrics

What does CTR stand for in digital advertising?

Why: CTR is the percentage of people who click an ad after seeing it (Clicks / Impressions).
Question 8 of 20PPC

In Google Ads, what determines your Ad Rank at auction time?

Why: Ad Rank is determined by multiple real-time factors including bid, auction competitiveness, context, format impact, and ad/landing page quality. Quality Score (the 1-10 number) is a diagnostic tool, not a direct auction input. Official Reference
Question 9 of 20Social Media

What is 'organic reach'?

Why: Organic reach is driven by the platform's algorithm and user sharing, not ad spend.
Question 10 of 20Analytics

How is bounce rate defined in Google Analytics 4 (GA4)?

Why: In GA4, bounce rate is the percentage of sessions that were not "engaged sessions." An engaged session lasts longer than 10 seconds, has a key event, or has at least 2 pageviews. (The older UA definition was simply a single-page session without interaction.) Official Reference
Question 11 of 20Email

What is a key requirement of the CAN-SPAM Act?

Why: CAN-SPAM requires a visible unsubscribe link and a physical mailing address.
Question 12 of 20Content

What is a 'buyer persona'?

Why: Personas help marketers tailor content and messaging to specific audience segments.
Question 13 of 20CRO

What is A/B testing?

Why: A/B testing isolates variables to determine what drives higher conversion rates.
Question 14 of 20SEO

Why are backlinks important for SEO?

Why: High-quality backlinks are one of the strongest ranking factors in Google's algorithm.
Question 15 of 20Metrics

If you spend $100 and acquire 5 customers, what is your CPA (Cost Per Acquisition)?

Why: CPA = Total Spend / Total Acquisitions ($100 / 5 = $20).
Question 16 of 20Affiliate

How does affiliate marketing generally work?

Why: Affiliates are paid a performance-based commission for driving sales or leads.
Question 17 of 20Video

What is the primary benefit of adding captions to marketing videos?

Why: Most videos on social media are watched without sound, making captions crucial for engagement.
Question 18 of 20Strategy

What is the 'marketing funnel'?

Why: The funnel maps stages like Awareness, Consideration, and Decision.
Question 19 of 20SEO

What is the purpose of a robots.txt file?

Why: A robots.txt file manages crawler traffic to avoid overloading your site. It is not a mechanism to keep a webpage out of Google. To prevent indexing, you must use noindex tags. Official Reference
Question 20 of 20PPC

What is retargeting (or remarketing)?

Why: Retargeting uses cookies to re-engage users who showed initial interest.

Final tips

  • Memorize the metric formulas — calculation questions are easy marks once you know them cold
  • Know the difference between remarketing (past visitors) and prospecting (new audiences)
  • Review email terms: open rate, CTR, list segmentation, deliverability, CAN-SPAM/GDPR basics
