Lean Six Sigma & Sustainability: 15-Question Practice Test

Published Sep 27, 2026 Last reviewed Oct 18, 2026 15 questions By the SkillPrep editorial team

Unofficial study resource. These practice questions are independent study aids, not official exam content.

Process-improvement and sustainability courses often end each chapter with short quizzes that many learners find trickier than expected. This unofficial 15-question practice test covers the core ideas — root-cause analysis, Lean wastes, the PDCA cycle, stakeholder engagement and sustainability fundamentals — with an explanation for every answer.

About this practice test

  • Format: 15 multiple-choice questions, one best answer each
  • Scoring: instant, with a per-topic breakdown and explanations
  • Trap to watch: several questions ask which option is NOT correct — read carefully
  • Wording varies: different courses and frameworks word some ideas differently (for example, the pillars of sustainability). Where that matters, the explanation says which framing we follow.

Topics covered

  • Root-cause analysis — the 5-Whys technique
  • Lean fundamentals — gemba, continuous improvement, visual management, and the DOWNTIME wastes
  • PDCA cycle — Plan-Do-Check-Act for continuous improvement
  • Lean Six Sigma goal — process improvement, defect reduction, waste minimization
  • Sustainability — project goals and charters, stakeholder engagement, the pillars of sustainability, global interconnection, and the NAE's 14 Grand Challenges for Engineering

Process Improvement 5 questions

Question 1 of 15Process Improvement

Your team project is consistently running late. How can the 5-Whys technique be applied to this situation?

Correct answer: Ask why the project is running late multiple times until pinpointing poor initial planning as the root cause
5-Whys drills down through successive 'why' questions to the root cause instead of treating symptoms.
Question 2 of 15Process Improvement

Why is it important to align process improvements with organizational objectives?

Correct answer: Aligning process improvements with organizational objectives ensures that changes contribute to the wider goals and enhance overall effectiveness.
Improvements that ladder up to organizational goals actually move the business forward.
Question 3 of 15Process Improvement

You are reviewing your team's ongoing project. How should you involve stakeholders in the planning process to improve overall outcomes?

Correct answer: Engage stakeholders early to surface valuable information and foster ownership.
Early engagement uncovers insights and builds buy-in — approval-only involvement comes too late.
Question 4 of 15Process Improvement

What is the primary benefit of identifying value-added activities in a business process?

Correct answer: It helps in focusing efforts on tasks that directly impact customer satisfaction and product quality.
Value-added analysis concentrates effort where it matters to the customer.
Question 5 of 15Process Improvement

You are leading a project to transition to new technology in your department. What should you prioritize to minimize downtime and ensure a smooth implementation?

Correct answer: Provide necessary training and resources for the team.
Trained, resourced teams adopt new technology with minimal disruption.

Sustainability 6 questions

Question 6 of 15Sustainability

What is the first step in implementing a sustainability project?

Correct answer: Define the goal of the sustainability project.
You can't plan, staff, or measure what you haven't defined — start with the goal.

Lean Fundamentals 4 questions

Question 7 of 15Lean Fundamentals

Which of the following is not a core competency of Lean management?

Correct answer: energy savings
Gemba (go see), kaizen (continuous improvement), and visual management are Lean staples; energy savings is an outcome, not a core competency.

Sustainability 6 questions

Question 8 of 15Sustainability

What are the three different contexts of sustainability?

Correct answer: social, environmental, economic
The standard three pillars (the 'triple bottom line') are social, environmental and economic — people, planet, profit. Some courses word the contexts differently, but this is the widely used framing.
Question 9 of 15Sustainability

What is one key strategy for building strong stakeholder relationships to improve sustainability performance?

Correct answer: Engage stakeholders through regular and transparent communication.
Regular, transparent communication is the foundation of stakeholder trust.

Lean Fundamentals 4 questions

Question 10 of 15Lean Fundamentals

What is the primary purpose of the PDCA cycle in business processes?

Correct answer: to implement and monitor continuous improvements
Plan-Do-Check-Act is the engine of continuous improvement.
Question 11 of 15Lean Fundamentals

Which statement best describes the primary goal of Lean Six Sigma?

Correct answer: to sustain results by improving processes, reducing defects, and minimizing waste
Lean Six Sigma = better processes + fewer defects + less waste, sustained over time.
Question 12 of 15Lean Fundamentals

Which of the following is not one of the eight types of waste identified by the DOWNTIME acronym in Lean methodology?

Correct answer: Innovation
DOWNTIME = Defects, Overproduction, Waiting, Non-utilized talent, Transportation, Inventory, Motion, Extra-processing. Innovation is not waste.

Sustainability 6 questions

Question 13 of 15Sustainability

Which statement best describes the interconnection between health, commerce, business, industry, government, and education in the context of global sustainability?

Correct answer: They are all closely linked and affect each other in various ways, influencing global sustainability efforts.
Sustainability is a web — every sector influences and depends on the others.
Question 14 of 15Sustainability

Which of the following is not one of the 14 grand challenges for engineering?

Correct answer: develop advanced AI for entertainment
The US National Academy of Engineering's 14 Grand Challenges include engineering better medicines, securing cyberspace and providing access to clean water. 'Advanced AI for entertainment' is not on the list.
Question 15 of 15Sustainability

What is the first step in creating a project charter for a sustainability initiative?

Correct answer: Define the project's goals, scope, and key customer requirements.
A charter starts with goals, scope, and customer requirements — everything else follows.

After the test: a study checklist

  • Memorize the DOWNTIME wastes — Defects, Overproduction, Waiting, Non-utilized talent, Transportation, Inventory, Motion, Extra-processing
  • Learn the three pillars of sustainability: social, environmental and economic (people, planet, profit)
  • Be able to name several of the NAE Grand Challenges — better medicines, secure cyberspace and clean water are all on the list
  • On "which is NOT" questions, eliminate the three true statements first
